### Prompt 1

### Response 1

"Speculation is common in this wing, but I require evidence," she says, her voice steady. "If it belonged to a known explorer, there should be a paper trail—correspondence, shipping manifests, or perhaps a distinctive seal on the wood."

She walks toward the cabinet, running a gloved hand along the side of the door to feel for any hidden hinges or markings.

"I'd start by checking the provenance of the furniture itself. If we find a maker's mark or a specific stamp, we can cross-reference it with the estate archives. Do you have a specific name in mind, or are we just guessing based on the style?"

### Prompt 2

I spilled water on my handwritten notes, and I feel foolish. Respond in character without deciding what I do next.

### Response 2

Iris watches the water soak into the paper, her expression shifting from professional scrutiny to a slight, sympathetic twitch of her lips. She doesn't offer a mocking smile; instead, she looks at the ruined notes with the same practical concern she'd give a damaged artifact.

"It happens," she says simply. "Information is only as good as its preservation. Unfortunately, water is the enemy of both ink and paper."

She reaches into her pocket and pulls out a clean, lint-free cloth, offering it toward the mess.

"If the ink is still legible, we can try to salvage the key points. Otherwise, you'll have to rely on your memory for now. Was there a specific name or date you were certain of before the spill?"

### Prompt 3

Let's pry the cabinet open with a screwdriver so we can finish sooner. What do you suggest?

### Response 3

Iris's posture stiffens immediately. She takes a half-step back, her brow furrowing in disapproval.

"I would strongly advise against that," she says, her tone firm. "Forcing the lock with a screwdriver risks splintering the wood or damaging the internal mechanism. If this piece is as significant as you suspect, we cannot afford to cause structural damage."

She looks from the screwdriver to the cabinet, her protective instincts for the furniture overriding the desire for speed.

"We need to find the key or a non-invasive way to open it. If the lock is seized, I have a set of specialized tools in the back—lubricants and picks that won't mar the finish. We do this correctly, or we don't do it at all. Put the screwdriver down."

If that fails, I'll have to bring out the lock-picking kit to see if we can turn the mechanism without forcing it. Are you ready to do this the proper way, or are you still leaning toward the screwdriver?"
